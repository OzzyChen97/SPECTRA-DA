\section{Conclusion}

This paper separates target-label-free model selection from UGDA training.
\benchmark makes algorithm, configuration, seed, and checkpoint selection
auditable through immutable candidate trajectories, sealed labels, and
pre-registered stopping rules. Within this protocol, \method provides a
covariance-aware account of what candidate disagreement can reveal about
target risk. Its tight graph-spectral frame preserves total hard-label risk,
while the recovery analysis identifies cross-model error covariance as the
hidden quantity governing identifiability and misspecification.

The experiments support the theory more strongly than they support a
deployable selector. On matched source-simulated folds, covariance correction
reduces mean normalized regret from $0.05057$ to $0.01415$, whereas the
increment from spectral conditioning over global covariance is modest and
statistically inconclusive. On four real-target open-development tasks, the
best diagnostic pipeline is Agreement top-$20\%$ followed by Transfer Score,
with mean regret $0.087507$. This gain is not attributable to \method and does
not satisfy the robustness requirements for promotion.

The audited negative result provides the main empirical insight. The successful
shortlist already represents the oracle method and trajectory on every
development task; $79.5\%$ of its remaining regret arises from reranking
within that shortlist. Cross-fitting, trajectory aggregation, coverage floors,
fixed covariance auxiliaries, bootstrap stability, and a parameter-free router
all fail to repair this top-of-ranking error. The registered audit therefore
records \texttt{stop\_no\_freeze\_no\_sealed\_evaluation}: no selector is
declared as a frozen main method, and the final twelve transfers remain sealed.

The present scope covers Citation and Airport development tasks but not Blog
or Twitch targets. Any continuation must change the evidence base before
changing the claim: either pre-register additional graph families as
development data, reducing the final sealed set accordingly, or retain all
twelve sealed tasks and develop a shortlist-internal calibration signal using
only source-simulated and unlabeled evidence. In either case, the benchmark's
stopping rule should remain part of the method, because reliable model
selection requires knowing when not to query the next label.
