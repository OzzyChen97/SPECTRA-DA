\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised graph domain adaptation (UGDA) is usually evaluated as a training
problem, yet deployment also requires choosing an algorithm, hyperparameter,
seed, and checkpoint without target labels. This selection problem is obscured
when public training loops report the best target-domain score. We introduce
\benchmark, a sealed-label trajectory protocol for target-label-free UGDA
model selection, and \method, a covariance-aware risk-recovery selector built
from graph-spectral agreement. Our key observation is that observable pairwise
disagreement equals the sum of individual risks minus cross-model error
covariance; a tight target-graph spectral frame decomposes this identity into
frequency bands without changing the total hard-label risk. We derive
covariance-controlled recovery bounds and estimate the missing covariance from
labeled source-simulated graph shifts matched to the target through unlabeled
descriptors. Nonnegative recovery produces a target-label-free risk score,
while spectral bands are used primarily to expose heterogeneous covariance
regimes. On a frozen development evaluation with four tasks, 44 held-out
source-simulated shift folds, and 675 candidates per task, \method reduces mean
normalized regret from $0.02278$ to $0.01415$ ($37.9\%$ relative), reaches
median Kendall $\tau=0.933$ and a $95.5\%$ top-$5\%$ hit rate, and records zero
label accesses or protocol violations. Ablation identifies covariance
correction as the main empirical driver; the incremental top-1 benefit of
spectral conditioning is modest and statistically inconclusive. A separate
one-time real-target development comparison shows that Transfer Score remains
stronger in top-1 selection ($0.1467$ versus $0.2560$ normalized regret), even
though our method has higher rank correlation. This result exposes
top-of-ranking calibration and cross-family covariance transport reliability
as unresolved limitations; we therefore make no state-of-the-art claim before
the pending sealed 16-transfer evaluation.
\end{abstract}
